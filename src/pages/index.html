Time it first. Count it second. Read the letters last.

Most guides start with the letters. This one thinks that is backwards. At night you see a point of light that comes and goes, and the surest thing you can measure is how long it takes to return to where it started: start a count at the first flash of a group, stop at the first flash of the next. That number is the period, and charts print it last, as 10s. Then count the flashes in a group. Only then match what you saw to the letters. The timer does it in that order: hold a button while a light shines, and it writes the characteristic.

The test is below. Hatteras, Portland Head and Fastnet all read Fl W: a single white flash. Only their periods, 7.5, 4 and 5 seconds, tell them apart.

The rule has limits. On the tip of Cape Cod, two neighbouring lights share a period, and only colour tells them apart.
