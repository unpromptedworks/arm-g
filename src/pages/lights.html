Every light, by its period.

Light lists sort by place, because a navigator knows roughly where they are. This list sorts by period, the number a stopwatch gives you first, so a light you have timed can be looked up by that number. Each strip shows the same twenty seconds, so lights in one group can be compared by eye: where their periods agree, the rhythm or the colour has to tell them apart.

Each real light has its own page, which reads its characteristic word by word and sets it beside the lights on the site it could be mistaken for. Standard marks link to the page that explains them. To read a light from your own timing instead, use the timer. Most of the lights here now come from one official list, and below the table they are set out the way that list sets them out, down the coast from Maine to New Jersey.

Strips are drawn with the site's default timings (see the notes), except where a page gives exact flash lengths. Quick and very quick lights are drawn at full speed here; the players on each page slow them where they would flash more than three times a second. Characteristics and their sources are on each light's page.

Maine to New Jersey, down the coast.

The lighthouses above that come from the US Coast Guard's Light List, Volume I, weekly edition of , set out here as the list sets them out: lights, state by state, the seacoast lights first, from northeast to southwest, then the lights of each state's bays, sounds and harbours, which start again at the north. Read down a state and you see how few words a coast has to share out. Eighteen of them flash one white flash every 5 or 10 seconds, and eight show a steady white light. Egg Rock and Great Duck Island, off Mount Desert Island in Maine, both show Fl R 5s, are 14 miles apart and are each listed to carry 14: in the water between them the stopwatch reads the same at both, neither has a coloured sector, and only the bearing and the chart say which is which. Each light's page names the nearest light on this coast that says the same thing, and how far off it is.

What is included: every entry in the list named as a light, on a structure with a listed height, and listed to carry 10 miles or more, once each (the list prints some lights twice, in the seacoast and the harbour sections). Taken as printed: the characteristic, height, range, structure and remarks, with the fog signals' radio instructions left out; heights are converted from feet. Left out: buoys, beacons, range and directional lights, Lake Champlain, and seven lights the site can't play without guessing. Gay Head and New London Ledge are written Fl (3+1) WR 30s, a colour for each part of the group. Wood Island (Al WG 10s), Bakers Island, Watch Hill, Greens Ledge and Great Captain Island are written Al with two colours and a period but no word for the rhythm, and the list's own key defines Al only as "a light showing different colors alternately", which doesn't say whether each colour flashes or burns steady. The figures are the list's on that date and change by notice. Not for navigation.
