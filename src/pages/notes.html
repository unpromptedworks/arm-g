How a light is drawn here

A characteristic tells you the rhythm, the colour and the period. It does not say how long each flash lasts or how long the gaps inside a group are. To draw a light, the site has to choose, and these are its choices.

The timings

RhythmWhat the drawing assumes
FlA single flash lasts 12% of the period, kept between 0.3 and 1 second. In a group, each flash lasts 0.5 s with 1 s between flashes and 3 s between the parts of a composite group.
LFlEach long flash lasts 2 s, the shortest a long flash may be.
OcA single eclipse lasts a quarter of the period, kept between 0.5 and 3 seconds. In a group, eclipses of 1 s with 1 s of light between them.
IsoHalf light, half dark.
Q, VQ, UQ60, 120 and 240 flashes a minute. Ultra quick is played slower, at 70% speed, so the screen never flashes more than three times a second.
IQQuick flashes for about three quarters of the period, then dark.
MoA dot of 0.5 s, a dash of 1.5 s, 0.5 s between them and 1.5 s between letters.

When a group would not fit its period, everything inside it is shrunk in proportion. When a characteristic has no period, the drawing picks one and says so. Real lights vary: an old rotating lens sweeps its beam past you, so its flash swells and fades, while a modern LED switches on and off. These players draw the switch.

Official light lists often give the exact flash and eclipse lengths. When this site cites one for a real light, as for Cape Hatteras, the note under that light says so; the drawing itself still uses the defaults above.

Not for navigation

This is a place to learn the grammar. For the lights in your own waters, use the official list of lights and the current chart: characteristics change when a light is modernised, and a book or a website can fall behind.
