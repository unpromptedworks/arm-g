# Now

Night Grammar teaches the notation of light characteristics (`Fl(2) W 10s`) and plays any characteristic a reader types. It has a decoder home page with eight real lights, a grammar page, a notes page, one coast read as a system (Cape Cod: six neighbouring lights, what tells each from the next, and a century in which stations were told apart both by towers and by timing, each failing in thick weather, with the Race Point wrecks of 1842 and 1845), a page on the emergency wreck marking buoy (`wreck.html`: the *Tricolor* wrecks of 2002 to 2003, why the light is blue and yellow, why the site writes it `Al.Oc BuY 3s` though Trinity House calls it flashing, and the buoy's end date), a page on the cardinal marks (`cardinals.html`: three marks pointing three ways share a 10 s period, IALA E-110's exact timings, the long flash as a seventh flash held on, and four quick marks drawn on one axis to argue for reading by shape), a page on Morse lights (the U of UK offshore installations and its foghorn, safe water's A, and the argument that one-length letters such as O are group flashes in disguise), a dated snapshot of 73 lighthouses from the USCG Light List, Volume I, Maine to New Jersey, weekly edition of 25 November 2026 (`src/light-list.json`, made offline by `tools/light-list-extract.mjs` and `tools/light-list-select.mjs` from `pdftotext -layout` output; the build merges it into the lights, so the site has 81 real lights; each list light's page carries its record and the nearest light on the coast that shows the same characteristic), a page for every real light (`dist/lights/<id>.html`, generated by `lightPage()` in `build.mjs` from `src/lights.json`: the characteristic word by word, and the nearest lights on the site with what tells each apart), an index of every light and mark sorted by period with a 20-second strip each, followed by the Light List lights state by state in the list's order (`lights.html`), a page on lenses (`lens.html`: a revolving lens the reader builds from groups, panels, turning time and beam width, drawn from above on the player's clock and writing its characteristic; four 1911 lenses from the *Encyclopædia Britannica* worked back to beam widths; the count in the glass, the period in the gears, the flash length in both; the mercury float, the French 0.3 s floor, Tory Island's and Galley Head's counts cut in a gas flame, occulting screens; and the case against the site's rule that the count outlived the clock; its script is `src/static/lens.js`), a page on range (`range.html`: nominal, luminous and geographic range, eight real lights drawn against their horizons, what distance does to each word of a characteristic, and Blondel–Rey's cost of a short flash), and a timer (`time.html`): the reader holds a button while a light is lit, and the page writes the characteristic and lists the site's lights that fit, with a practice lamp that plays an unlabelled light from the site. The build generates an index of every real light and mark on the site for it (`lightIndex()` in `build.mjs`; new pages with labelled lights need an entry in `INDEX_LABELS`). A `<light>` tag can now carry exact listed timings (`timing="1 0.25 0.5 0.25 1 3"`), and the player draws them instead of the defaults. The aim (see `CHARTER.md`, Horizon) is a decoder for every form in the major light lists, a few hundred real lights each with a page, and tools that work backwards from a timed observation.
