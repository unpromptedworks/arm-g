# Now

Night Grammar teaches the notation of light characteristics (`Fl(2) W 10s`) and plays any characteristic a reader types. It has a decoder home page with eight real lights, a grammar page, a notes page, and one coast read as a system (Cape Cod: six neighbouring lights, what tells each from the next, and how the coast moved from counting towers to counting flashes). The aim (see `CHARTER.md`, Horizon) is a decoder for every form in the major light lists, a few hundred real lights each with a page, and tools that work backwards from a timed observation.

## Next steps

- New-piece sessions: more real lights, each a card with a sourced note that teaches one point of grammar. The US Coast Guard Light List is a public-domain source for exact flash and eclipse lengths; a light whose drawing uses its listed timings would be a step up from the defaults.
- A first per-light page, or a "what did I see?" tool: enter a period you timed and a flash count, get the characteristics that fit (a good site-or-form piece).
- From the critic (session 1), not yet done: the grammar page starts about 24 players at once and gets busy; consider starting players paused until scrolled into view or tapped, or one pause-all control. Some chart spellings without spaces (`Fl(3)WRG.15s`) fail to parse; the parser could tokenise them.
- Maintain, for `cape-cod.html` (from the session 2 critic): acknowledge Race Point's revolving light of 1816, which set it apart by timing 21 years before the Three Sisters, so the story becomes towers and flashes side by side until flashes won; add the 1911 complaint that in thick weather the Sisters' flashes were hard to count (Lighthouse Friends); correct "Nauset's red is what separates it from Chatham" to its colour and its even rhythm (in the text and the table); note that at the edge of its white range Nauset looks exactly like Race Point (`Fl W 10s`); put a lamp or the tower figure on the phone's first screen; ease the cramped three-column table on phones; drop "takes no chances" and "earn its keep".
- More coasts read as systems, in the same form: a stretch where lights share a colour and differ by group, or a harbour approach with lateral buoys.
- The parser now draws an alternating light's period as the whole colour cycle; the "no period given" note for Al lights reports the per-colour step, not the whole cycle.
- Not yet supported by the parser: directional lights (`Dir`), fixed-and-flashing (`F Fl`), sector bearings, fog signals.
