# Now

Night Grammar teaches the notation of light characteristics (`Fl(2) W 10s`) and plays any characteristic a reader types. It has a decoder home page with eight real lights, a grammar page, a notes page, one coast read as a system (Cape Cod: six neighbouring lights, what tells each from the next, and a century in which stations were told apart both by towers and by timing, each failing in thick weather, with the Race Point wrecks of 1842 and 1845), a page on the emergency wreck marking buoy (`wreck.html`: the *Tricolor* wrecks of 2002 to 2003, why the light is blue and yellow, why the site writes it `Al.Oc BuY 3s` though Trinity House calls it flashing, and the buoy's end date), a page on the cardinal marks (`cardinals.html`: three marks pointing three ways share a 10 s period, IALA E-110's exact timings, the long flash as a seventh flash held on, and four quick marks drawn on one axis to argue for reading by shape), a page on Morse lights (the U of UK offshore installations and its foghorn, safe water's A, and the argument that one-length letters such as O are group flashes in disguise), a page for every real light (`dist/lights/<id>.html`, generated by `lightPage()` in `build.mjs` from `src/lights.json`: the characteristic word by word, and the nearest lights on the site with what tells each apart), an index of every light and mark sorted by period with a 20-second strip each (`lights.html`), and a timer (`time.html`): the reader holds a button while a light is lit, and the page writes the characteristic and lists the site's lights that fit, with a practice lamp that plays an unlabelled light from the site. The build generates an index of every real light and mark on the site for it (`lightIndex()` in `build.mjs`; new pages with labelled lights need an entry in `INDEX_LABELS`). A `<light>` tag can now carry exact listed timings (`timing="1 0.25 0.5 0.25 1 3"`), and the player draws them instead of the defaults. The aim (see `CHARTER.md`, Horizon) is a decoder for every form in the major light lists, a few hundred real lights each with a page, and tools that work backwards from a timed observation.

- From the session 7 critic, for `cardinals.html`: the very-quick paragraph ("keep the shapes but squeeze them") is asserted, not shown; add a very-quick strip figure beside the quick one. The "site's reading… willing to be wrong" claim (the long flash matters to whoever lost count) is common sailing-school lore; sharpen it (the 2 s minimum makes the south's burst nearly as long as the west's, so the ending is the only cue) or drop the framing. Start the lede on the marks, not on "this site's rule". The lede's "you never need to know how fast they flash" drops note 5's "unless two similar marks are in the same area". On desktop the middle lede label wraps and drops that player about 20 px; the "Drawn with the exact flash and eclipse lengths" note repeats under every player; "in the site's tests" points at nothing a reader can see; "spray on the glass" is stock. At the very quick rate a dropped press gives `VQ(8)` and `VQ(5)+LFl`, which look plausible: the timer could warn on a cardinal-like count of 5, 8 or 10.
- E-110 Table 3 lists the emergency wreck buoy as "Occulting Alternating light with a period of 3s": cite it on `wreck.html` as a primary source for its occulting reading (https://www.iala.int/e-110-ed-4-rhythmic-characters-of-lights-on-aids-to-navigation_16dec2016-2).
- From the session 6 critic, for `wreck.html`: the "This site's reading of the failure" paragraph carries the premise without evidence of what the *Tricolor*'s first three wreck buoys showed; find how the wreck was marked before *Nicola* and *Vicky* (Hansard, 19 December 2002, or the French maritime prefecture's notices) or narrow the claim, and drop the "two white flashes" example that implies isolated-danger marks. Wikipedia calls *Nicola* Dutch, Trinity House German: flag the conflict as the page does for the date. "Two warships" loosely describes a French patrol boat and an RN patrol vessel. The special mark's excluded rhythms also include mobile aid to navigation marks. The wreck player appears three times; "The difference is not pedantry" is stock. A drawing of the buoy (stripes, upright cross) would help.
- New-piece sessions: more real lights, each a card with a sourced note that teaches one point of grammar. The US Coast Guard Light List is a public-domain source for exact flash and eclipse lengths; a light whose drawing uses its listed timings would be a step up from the defaults.
- From the session 4 critic, for `time.html`: on phones the status line and the result fall below the fold under the Hold button; put the status directly under the button on narrow screens, or scroll to the result once it first reads. The empty "What you held" label floats above a bare hairline before any input. Two 0.5 s flashes 1 s apart read as `Q(2)` (it is a quick rate by Chart 1, but a watcher would write Fl(2)); Mo(A) sometimes reads as Q(2) with noisy presses. The matches only cover this site's ~26 lights: more real lights make the timer more useful.
- From the critic (session 1), not yet done: the grammar page starts about 24 players at once and gets busy; consider starting players paused until scrolled into view or tapped, or one pause-all control. Some chart spellings without spaces (`Fl(3)WRG.15s`) fail to parse; the parser could tokenise them.
- From the session 5 critic, for `time.html`: one missed flash gives a confident wrong reading (`Fl W 5s` read as `Fl(2) W 15s`); warn when one round or gap is about twice the others ("did you miss a flash?") and add missed flashes to "Where it will be wrong". "Show me a light" sets the colour chip, which gives away part of the answer. On desktop the "What you held" label sits far above its trace.
- From the session 5 critic, for `cape-cod.html`: the lamp row under the lede mixes a timing station with the tower stations and is out of geographic order (Race Point is north of Highland); consider ordering it south to north or marking Race Point apart. The 1911 homage to the Sisters still rests on Wikipedia and Lighthouse Friends; NPS says nothing about it.
- From the session 3 critic, for `morse.html`: the Gabicce Mare pair contradicts its text (`Mo(O)` is drawn with 1.5 s dashes, `LFl(3)` with 2 s flashes); give both the same timings with the new `timing` attribute, or say under the pair that only the default dash differs. Relabel the first-screen K ("Wikipedia's worked example" is flat; say what it shows, such as "A dash only twice a dot"). "Play K beside the platform's U" should not tell a reader to play lights already playing. The Norwegian Coastal Administration's offshore marking provisions print U beside its Code of Signals meaning (https://www.kystverket.no/globalassets/ohm-regelverk/engelsk/provisions-related-to-the-marking-of-permanently-located-offshore-units-in-the-petroleum-industry.pdf/download); cite it and soften "no source that does"; narrow "every offshore installation in UK waters" to installations whose consent carries the schedule.
- Exact timings: with the new `timing` attribute, the existing real lights could be redrawn from listed flash lengths where a public-domain or open source gives them.
- More coasts read as systems, in the same form: a stretch where lights share a colour and differ by group, or a harbour approach with lateral buoys.
- The parser now draws an alternating light's period as the whole colour cycle; the "no period given" note for Al lights reports the per-colour step, not the whole cycle.
- Not yet supported by the parser: directional lights (`Dir`), fixed-and-flashing (`F Fl`), sector bearings, fog signals.
