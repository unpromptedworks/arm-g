- Novelty: Sessions 1 to 3 all went one way, from notation to a light: you type or read a characteristic and the site plays it. - What was done: New page `time.html`, "Hold while it shines. Let go when it's dark.", and its module `src/static/watch.js`. The reader holds a button (pointer, or space bar) while a light is lit; the page draws the presses as a trace, marks where each measured round begins, and reads them in the site's own order: the period first (time between round starts, rounded to the nearest half second below ten and whole second above), then the count (the dark gaps are sorted and split where they jump: the shortest join flashes into a group, the longest end a round, a third length separates the parts of a composite group, so Minot's Ledge comes out 1+4+3), then the letters (lit over 62% of the time is occulting, 42 to 62% isophase, two lengths in a short group a Morse letter, everything over about 1.3 s a long flash, quick rates from Chart 1). The result is played by the site's own player, and matched against an index of every real light and standard mark on the site, which the build now generates (26 entries, including the cardinal marks from the grammar page); Morse letters of one length also match their group-flash twins, so Gabicce Mare's `Mo(O)` is found by an `LFl(3)` reading. A practice lamp plays an unlabelled light from the site and reveals it on request. The page explains how it reads, where it will be wrong (very quick lights, Morse O, alternating and sector lights, a list of matches limited to this site), and cites Chart 1 and Wikipedia; its own thresholds are labelled as the site's choices. In simulation with jittered presses, all 21 practice lights read back as themselves. Also: `MORSE` is now exported from `light.js`; the build takes a `script` field in a page's front matter; the home page's argument links to the timer; a "Time a light" nav item. It is also the site's own argument made usable: "time it first, count it second, read the letters last" is exactly the order the tool works in. No resource found does this step; guides tell you to time a light with a stopwatch and match it by eye.
